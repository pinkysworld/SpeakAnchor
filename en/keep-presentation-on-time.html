Keep to your speaking time: how to stay on schedule

If you overrun, you lose the audience. If you finish far too early, you prepared slides for nothing. A few simple habits get you close to the time most of the time.

1. Set a time for each section

Split the talk into sections and give each a number of minutes. That way you know after the first third whether you are ahead or behind, not five minutes before the end.

2. Rehearse once out loud with a stopwatch

Read the whole talk aloud at least once and time it. Silent run-throughs are almost always faster than speaking. In front of a live audience it usually takes a little longer, so plan a buffer, for example a few minutes in a half-hour talk.

3. Word count as a rough guide

Speaking freely is roughly 120 to 150 words per minute, depending on the person and topic. That is only a rule of thumb. Measure your own pace while rehearsing and you get a reliable figure for your text.

4. Cut rather than speed up

If the talk is too long, remove content instead of talking faster. Mark in advance the passages you can skip when short on time, so you do not have to decide on the fly.

5. Use a timer that does not distract

An ordinary clock tends to distract. A timer that shows remaining time and warns with colours, such as green, yellow, red, helps. Even better is a per-section comparison: am I ahead of or behind my plan?

Frequently asked questions

How much longer does a talk take in front of an audience than in rehearsal?

Usually a bit longer, because of reactions, pauses and questions. A buffer of a few minutes makes sense. There is no fixed percentage that fits everyone.

How many words fit into 10 minutes?

As a rough rule of thumb about 1,200 to 1,500 words when speaking freely. It is better to measure your own pace.

What if I fall behind mid-talk?

Skip the passages you marked in advance and summarise the next section more briefly. That works better than rushing.
